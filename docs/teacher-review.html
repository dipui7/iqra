AlifUp — teacher review sheet

Thank you for checking this. AlifUp is a free app that teaches adult beginners to read the Quran. Below is every piece of teaching content in the app — letter names, sounds, explanations, rules and recordings — exactly as learners see it. Please tick OK or write a correction. The Quran text itself (Tanzil Uthmani, unchanged) and the recitation (Mishary Alafasy) are not in question here.

How sounds are written: one Latin letter (or pair) per Arabic letter — ḥ ṣ ḍ ṭ ẓ for the heavy letters, ʿ for ʿayn, th dh kh gh sh as in the names; long vowels ā ī ū. The hints are deliberately short and in plain English for adult beginners.

3. Reading rules

Practice syllables are put together from letters and marks, as in a Qāʿida (e.g. مَكْ "mak", رَبَّ "rabba", بًا "ban", بَا "bā"). Wāw and yā are never given sukūn after a matching vowel (that would be a long vowel), and ʿayn is left out of two-syllable practice words.

RuleExplanation shown to learnersCheck
SukūnA small circle above a letter: the letter has no vowel of its own. It closes the syllable before it: m-a-k is read "mak". OK
Fix:
ShaddaA small "w" above a letter: say the letter twice — hold it, then add its vowel: r-a-bb-a is read "rabba". OK
Fix:
TanwīnA doubled vowel mark adds an "n" sound: "an", "in", "un". It comes at the end of a word; the "an" form is usually followed by an alif, which is not read. OK
Fix:
Long vowelsAlif after fatḥa, yā after kasra and wāw after ḍamma stretch the vowel to two beats: "bā", "bī", "bū". In the Quran a small alif above a letter does the same. OK

6. Voice questions in exams

The learner records themselves and the app compares the recording with the reciter's, on the device. It is told:

Your voice is compared with the reciter's recording on this device and never uploaded. It checks how alike they sound, not tajweed.

Is this wording clear enough that nobody takes it as approval of their recitation? OK
